% Sidenote Rules:
% The revised rule for your dissertation

% A sidenote should give the reader one useful extra thought at the exact point where it becomes relevant.

% It should usually be:

% locally anchored;
% one idea only;
% one or two sentences;
% concrete rather than comprehensive;
% optional to the main argument.

% The main text carries the claim. The sidenote may carry its:

% source;
% example;
% qualification;
% terminological gloss;
% cross-reference;
% occasional aside.
% What your sidenotes should not become

% Avoid using them for:

% full theoretical definitions;
% mini literature reviews;
% complete methodological explanations;
% mappings between every RQ, chapter, and contribution;
% substantial justification for choosing biophilic design or sound;
% essential limitations;
% long lists of technical details.

% For example, the distinction among emotion, affective lens, and Affective Interaction is conceptually central. It should be established in the main text, perhaps with one citation or short historical clarification in the margin. A three-part definition entirely inside a sidenote would be too much.

% Similarly, this would be too administrative for a Turon-style note:

% C1 is developed in Chapters 2 and 3; C2 in Chapters 4 and 5...

% That belongs in the synthesis or chapter overview, not necessarily in the margin.
