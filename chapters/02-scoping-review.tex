\chapter{Specifying Human Experience in Interactive Indoor Environments}\label{ch:study-one}

\begin{synopsis}
\synopsislead{What forms of human experience}{are recognised and designed for in research on interactive indoor environments? Research in this area spans HCI, architecture, building science, environmental psychology, and related fields, each contributing different concepts, priorities, and methods for understanding experience. Mapping this interdisciplinary landscape is essential for clarifying the experiential concerns represented across the field, the design approaches through which they have been addressed, and the directions that require further investigation.}

This chapter develops this account through a scoping review of 192 papers published between 1996 and 2024 and retrieved from the ACM Digital Library and Scopus. Using a hybrid deductive--inductive coding approach, the review identifies 11 targeted human experiences, 20 design mechanisms, and two typologies of technological intervention.

Within the trajectory of the dissertation, this review provides the broad conceptual foundation from which the subsequent studies develop. It addresses \rqref{scoping} by specifying which forms of human experience have been pursued in research on interactive indoor environments and how they have been approached through design and
technology. 

Emotional experience constitutes a substantial strand of this landscape, while its potential as a methodological entry point for investigating situated indoor experience remains underdeveloped. Biophilic design, as a design mechanism for developing these experiences appears as a comparatively less represented design
mechanism. These findings establish two directions developed later in the dissertation: affective inquiry into situated indoor experience and multisensory biophilic design for future interactive environments.
\end{synopsis}

\begin{chapterclosing}{What this chapter establishes}
The review specifies the forms of human experience currently addressed across research on interactive indoor environments, together with the design mechanisms and technological interventions through which they have been pursued.

Emotional experience constitutes a substantial strand of this research landscape, while biophilic design is comparatively less represented among the identified design mechanisms. Within the dissertation, these findings establish two directions for further investigation. The first uses emotion and Affective Interaction as an entry point for examining how spatial, environmental, technological, and social conditions acquire significance within particular situations. The second is developed later through multisensory biophilic design as an approach to future interactive indoor environments.

\chaptercarryforward{The following chapter develops the first of these directions through Affective Interaction, moving from a field-level mapping of human experience to a situated investigation of how indoor experience is sensed, interpreted, anticipated, and made meaningful.}
\end{chapterclosing}
