\chapter{Study One: Scoping the Landscape of Human Experience Research in Interactive Indoor Environments}\label{ch:study-one}

\begin{synopsis}
\synopsislead{What forms of human experience}{have already been considered or addressed in research on indoor environments? Although many studies describe the improvement of occupant experience as an objective, they differ considerably in what they understand experience to include and how they seek to influence it.}

The scoping review brings together research from HCI, architecture, building research, environmental psychology, and related fields. It examines the experiential aims pursued by this work, the design mechanisms used to address them, and the spatial or technological interventions developed.

Its purpose is not only to classify previous research, but to establish the experiential territory within which HBI operates.

The review shows that human-experience design in buildings extends beyond dominant concerns with comfort, usability, and environmental performance. It includes emotional, bodily, aesthetic, social, democratic, inclusive, educational, and place-related forms of experience. It also identifies a broad repertoire of design approaches, including spatial, social, participatory, personalising, informational, playful, technological, and biophilic mechanisms.

At the same time, the review identifies emotional experience as a promising but under-examined way of approaching indoor complexity. Emotion is present across parts of the literature, yet it is often treated as an outcome to be measured rather than as a means of understanding how people relate to their surroundings. The review therefore points to the need for closer examination of the methodological potential of an emotional and affective lens.

This finding establishes the basis for the subsequent occupant studies. Rather than beginning with the assumption that comfort or satisfaction provides the most appropriate account of indoor experience, these studies examine what becomes visible when emotion is used as an entry point into occupants' situated accounts.
\end{synopsis}

\section*{Chapter framing}

The dissertation begins by examining how human experience has been understood and addressed across research on interactive indoor environments. Human--Building Interaction acknowledges that experience in buildings is complex, but this complexity can remain insufficiently specified. Before adopting a particular theoretical or methodological lens, it is therefore necessary to establish what forms of experience have already been studied, how they have been approached through design, and what kinds of interactive technologies have been used to engage with them.

This chapter addresses that need through a scoping review of research at the intersection of human experience, interactive technologies, and indoor built environments. The review maps the experiential concerns represented across the field. It examines which forms of human experience researchers have sought to support, the design mechanisms through which these experiences have been approached, and the technological interventions used within buildings. In doing so, it provides a field-level account of the different ways in which HCI, HBI, architecture, and related areas have conceptualised the relationship between people, technologies, and indoor environments.

The review establishes the conceptual starting point from which the subsequent studies proceed. Its purpose is not yet to prescribe a particular approach to indoor experience, but to clarify the existing research landscape and identify where further investigation is needed. The paper reproduced in this chapter therefore addresses the first research question of the dissertation: what forms of human experience are already being addressed in research on interactive indoor environments, and through which design approaches?
