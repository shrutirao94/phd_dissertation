\chapter{Specifying Human Experience in Interactive Indoor Environments}\label{ch:study-one}

\begin{synopsis}
\synopsislead{What forms of human experience}{should HBI recognise as part of its research agenda? Research on interactive indoor environments spans HCI, architecture, building science, environmental psychology, and related fields, each contributing different concepts, priorities, and methods for understanding experience. Mapping this interdisciplinary landscape is essential for specifying what indoor experiential complexity consists of, how it has been approached through design, and which directions require further investigation.}

The paper presented in this chapter develops this account through a scoping review of 192 papers published between 1996 and 2024 and retrieved from the ACM Digital Library and Scopus. Using a hybrid deductive--inductive coding approach, the review identifies 11 targeted human experiences, 20 design mechanisms, and two typologies of technological intervention, providing a structured account of how research has connected human experience, interaction design, technology, and the built environment.

Within the trajectory of the dissertation, this review provides the broad conceptual foundation from which the subsequent studies develop. It addresses the first research question by specifying which forms of human experience have been pursued in research on interactive indoor environments and how they have been approached through design. The resulting landscape also identifies emotional experience as a significant and promising direction for examining how spatial, environmental, technological, and social conditions acquire meaning for occupants. This establishes the dissertation's first narrowing of focus: from the breadth of human experience addressed across the field towards the potential of an affective lens for understanding situated indoor experience.
\end{synopsis}

\begin{chapterclosing}{Concluding synthesis}

\chapterclosinglead{The review specifies}{the forms of human experience that comprise HBI's experiential agenda and the spatial, material, social, and technological approaches through which they have been addressed.}

Emotion emerges as both a substantial strand of existing research and a promising methodological entry point for investigating how occupants interpret and relate to their surroundings. Emotional accounts reveal how light, sound, visibility, enclosure, activity, technology, and social presence acquire significance within particular situations.

\chaptercarryforward{The following chapter develops this direction through Affective Interaction, moving from a field-level mapping of human experience to a situated investigation of how indoor experience is sensed, interpreted, anticipated, and made meaningful.}

\end{chapterclosing}
