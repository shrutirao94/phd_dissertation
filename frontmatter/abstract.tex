\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

How we experience indoor environments is shaped by emotion, prior experience, social context, and the conditions of everyday life. Human-Building Interaction (HBI) responds to this complexity by studying the relations between human experience, interactive technologies, and built environments. Yet the nature of this complexity remains under-specified. What kinds of experience matter indoors, how can HBI understand them, and how might this guide future interactive spaces? This thesis addresses these questions by investigating indoor experience through an \emph{affective lens} and developing biophilic approaches for designing future interactive spaces. It advances HBI through four contributions: a structured account of human-experience design research in interactive indoor environments, Affective Interaction as a methodological lens for revealing lived experience indoors, multisensory biophilic design as an approach for expanding the experiential possibilities of interactive indoor environments, and an AI-transformed indoor soundscape as a concrete design instance.

The thesis begins with a scoping review of human experience design research in indoor environments, identifying emotional experience and biophilic design as promising but under-examined directions for engaging with indoor complexity. It pursues these directions through situated occupant studies, expert interviews, expert design sessions, a public survey, and prototype demonstrations with experts. The occupant studies use Affective Interaction as a methodological lens to reveal how occupants sense, interpret, anticipate, and make meaning within socio-spatial contexts. The expert studies identify under-explored dimensions of biophilic experience in technologically mediated spaces and translate these insights into a design framework for HBI. The soundscape studies examine how AI-transformed office sound can support responsive, nature-like listening experiences that enrich quietness, mask activity sounds, and preserve environmental connection, while identifying the experiential and design conditions required for their integration into shared workplaces.
